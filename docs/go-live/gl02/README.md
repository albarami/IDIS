# GL-02 obligation register: runbook

This folder holds the public record of the GL-02 obligation register. It never holds fund wording. The register itself is private and lives on the coordinator's machine in a git-ignored directory. This page says what the register is, how it was checked, how to read the public manifest and how to run the tools. The design is `docs/plans/2026-10-20-gl02-obligation-register-design.md` (version 2.6); checkpoint 38 adds the validator's reusable typed-link correction (item 8 below).

## What the register is

The fund's financial workbook has 79 checklist items on four sheets (P&L, Cash Flow, Liabilities, Assets). The commercial template has 21 questions. Each of the 100 items is a parent. Each parent is broken into child obligations: one separately answerable thing that a finished analysis must establish, compute or judge, with quotes that cite the parent's exact wording, the evidence it needs, the calculation or judgment, the specialist, the destination in the report and an acceptance case. The sealed register holds all 100 parents in five shards with 954 child records (934 active, 20 retired), and it is the input to the later steps (GL-07 onward) that plan evidence collection and calculation for every item.

The register is a requirements artifact: it records what a complete answer needs, not the completed diligence answers. Every child stays `proposed` until the owner and the fund review. The scope is the full register — all 100 parents, all five shards, all components and, downstream, all four fund deliverables (the completed response workbook, the narrative report, the evidence and calculation annex, and the IC memo); the two-question view (`pilot_two_question`) is a diagnostic subset, not the delivery scope.

## Privacy rules

- The original workbook stays private at its existing location, unchanged — nothing of it is copied or moved. Everything generated for the register stays private and git-ignored: the derived files, the authored shards and the review pack live in the register's private root under `.local_reports/`, and the sealed snapshots sit next to it, outside the private root. The commercial template and the category map are already-public repository artifacts and remain in their existing repository locations; the formula-policy files and the matching review-map documents are private, git-ignored repository paths that stay private like the workbook — a path inside the repository is not a tracked or public file.
- Public documents carry identifiers, coordinates, counts, hashes and generic categories only. A coordinate is never paired with what its cell contains or asks.
- No copy of the register is made off this machine. A sealed snapshot is a local copy next to the private root, and worktree copies are preserved after a verified sync unless the owner asks for cleanup.
- Model providers: the authoring and review agents processed the workbook text under the owner's authorization of 2026-10-20; the agents of the current routing (OpenAI for Codex Sol, Cognition for Devin SWE 2 Max) process it where necessary under the owner's authorization of 2026-10-21 (AUTH-ROUTING-20261021). Confirmation that the fund's terms allow this stays the owner's decision (START_HERE, owner decision (c)).

## How the register was built and checked

1. The extractor reads the approved workbook once and writes the derived files (cells, parents, merged instructions, policy view) byte for byte, with the original wording kept.
2. Five shards (P&L, Cash Flow, Liabilities, Assets, Commercial) were authored through a Python script each, in rounds — the earlier rounds by Cursor/Grok agents, the continuation under AUTH-ROUTING-20261021 by Devin SWE 2 Max.
3. After every round an independent reviewer session reviewed the shard — an actual Opus 5.5 session until the routing change of 2026-10-21, afterwards the separate Sol review session, with every accepted review staying valid for the bytes it reviewed (verdict PASS when no high or medium finding is open; a low finding is applied in a last revision or listed in the status record). A separate omission-auditor session per shard compared the shard with its own blind list of what a complete answer needs, built from the source alone. Those original blind lists were created by the earlier independent auditor sessions; the continuation Sol auditor sessions compared the actual changed keys against the same unchanged lists and carried the proven unchanged coverage by exact identity — no blind list was rewritten and no key was silently re-audited. The final omission records cover all 100 parents with no gap and are byte-identical to the reviewed sources.
4. The validator checks structure and the wording accounting: every material stretch of source wording is quoted by a focused child or explicitly dispositioned; every shared and cross-row instruction, policy anchor and binding, plan-minimum code and named clarification is carried; every cited identifier resolves. For every active child whose parent is in the selected scope, each declared `ambiguity_ids` and `cross_row_instruction_ids` entry must resolve to exactly one loaded record of its type — the typed-link item below records how the earlier gap was found and closed. A skipped required check is reported as skipped and never counted as a pass.
5. Four full-register reviews read the five shards as one document (cross-sheet ties and duplicates, conflicting rules, conventions, policy carriage, decision owners, plan minimum, and the synthesis that no single sheet owns). The fourth review — by a separate Sol session under AUTH-ROUTING-20261021, after the routing change — checked 100 parents and 934 active children, sampled 54 actual cross-sheet citations, found the 39 earlier findings resolved and the 3 new findings resolved, and listed one low finding (an invalidated unconditional author self-check that receives no engine or full-rule proof credit, with all original artifacts retained). Closure then ran as a bounded re-review of the corrected bytes and their dependencies with exact identity carry — it supplemented the initial fourth full-register review and the earlier accepted lineage instead of replacing review 04 or repeating a complete register read after every fix.
6. The mutation checks broke copies of the real register on purpose (a deleted child, a changed quote, a removed disposition, a removed policy carry, a duplicated id, a child moved to another parent, and further cases) and showed the validator failing with the right code. The record keeps the classes separate: 55 initial attempts produced 42 expected error detections, and the no-error cases (11, one of them a no-op) and the inapplicable skips (2) received no pass credit; 13 supplemental validator cases were each detected with the expected code and no miss or skip.
7. The register was sealed on 2026-10-21 (sequence 1, tree `4555dd6b9ee847448336b8488ccc9c785b92272e01dabaecddb9e6ea6ba3aa55`): the ledger records every one of the 954 child identifiers ever issued, so a lost, moved or re-used identifier fails validation. Post-seal validation of the sealed register reports no finding and no skipped required check, and the six post-seal ledger cases on damaged copies were each detected with the expected primary code, again with no miss or skip.
8. One applicability gap is on record from the mutation runs: the validator of checkpoints 35 to 37 did not check every ambiguity and cross-row typed target (finding MUT-APP01-001 of the independent mutation-applicability review). For the first seal the check was supplied by a mandatory fail-closed private control that is independently reviewed and that ran against the exact sealed aggregate — it detected 28 missing links across 8 damaged copies and reported no finding on the canonical register — so its disposition stays MITIGATED_FOR_EXACT_PACKET for those historical bytes and is not rewritten. The reusable correction now lives inside the validator itself (checkpoint 38): for every active child whose parent is in the selected scope, each declared `ambiguity_ids` and `cross_row_instruction_ids` entry must resolve to exactly one loaded record of its type, counted over all loaded shard files so duplicates in one file or across shards are never collapsed — zero matches is `GL02_TYPED_LINK_UNRESOLVED`, two or more is `GL02_TYPED_LINK_AMBIGUOUS`, both I-08 errors that refuse sealing through the existing report path. The new typed-link findings publish bounded ASCII identifier tokens only — a declared token outside `[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9_.:-]{0,159}` is omitted from those findings, never echoed, while its resolution count still fails — and no authored-identifier grammar is imposed, so a legal unusual or Unicode id with exactly one record resolves normally. Unrelated legacy diagnostics are unchanged and may still print other identifiers — no whole-validator privacy hardening is claimed. Out-of-scope children gain no new finding, though their targets still resolve for in-scope children; retired children keep all pre-existing checks but are outside only this obligation; empty lists stay legal. A separate Sol code review passed the five accepted source/test paths with no finding, and the root's nine read-only private validations of the corrected CLI reproduced the canonical register's clean result and the same 28 missing links on the same 8 preserved copies, and the independent bounded Sol evidence review re-checked all nine cases' typed ids, fields and counts in place and by Counter, enumerated the actual registry (the 20 real invariant ids, I-06b included, I-20 excluded) and verified every input by hash (`sol-typed02-private-evidence-review.json` SHA256 e857f7d0…; RUN_STATE record 187).

## The public coverage manifest

`register-manifest.json` lists, for each of the 100 parents, the public coordinate, the child counts by derivation class and per-field counts (characters, words, covered characters, runs); counts for the whole register (children, quotes, dispositions, ambiguities by topic code, cross-row instructions); the pinned identity of the inputs (size and SHA-256 of the workbook, the policy file, the template and the category map); the file-level hashes of the private files; one digest per sheet; the status of each invariant the validator reports (20 checks, all pass in this artifact); and the ledger state (sealed, sequence, tree hash). A scan rejects any key outside an allowlist or any value outside the exact format for its key, so no text can reach it.

The published artifact is not the output of the bare `manifest` CLI command: that command takes no workbook or policy argument, so it leaves the workbook-wording check I-14 skipped, and its output must not be relabelled as this artifact. The published artifact was produced by the qualified recipe — `load_register`, then `validate_register` with the original workbook and the policy file given, then `assemble_public_manifest`, `assert_public_safe` and `public_manifest_bytes` — and the repeated assembly produced identical bytes (SHA-256 `5c2d9a4453df752d93b048349f9521b89065014158571c19ab6f1b49fb5c5ebe`, 103,902 bytes) with the source and ledger hashes unchanged. After the typed-link correction the root re-ran the qualified recipe with the corrected validator and produced byte-identical output — same SHA-256, same 103,902 bytes (RUN_STATE record 187).

## Commands

All commands run from a checkout with `PYTHONPATH=src` and the repository interpreter: `python scripts/gl02_register.py <command> --private-root <root>`.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `extract --workbook <xlsx> [--policy <json>]` | Writes the derived files (private root). |
| `validate [--workbook <xlsx>] [--policy <json>] [--shard X ...] [--view V] [--format json]` | Runs the invariants; exit code 1 on any error. Give the workbook and the policy for a complete result. |
| `status [--shard X ...]` | Per-shard counts, no text. |
| `runs --parent <coordinate> [--show-text]` | Lists the segments, runs and markers of one parent (text only with `--show-text`). |
| `quote --parent <coordinate> --text <text>` | Resolves a quote against a parent's wording. |
| `manifest [--private-out F] [--public-out F]` | Writes the private and the public-safe manifests; the subcommand accepts no workbook or policy argument, so its output leaves the I-14 check skipped (see the manifest section above). |
| `seal [--workbook ...] [--policy ...] [--shard X ...]` | Validates and seals; refuses on any error, writes nothing on refusal. |
| `views` | Lists the views (the two-question diagnostic view is `pilot_two_question`, proposed). |
| `review-pack [--out-dir D] [--shard X ...]` | Writes the private reading pack (markdown and one workbook per shard) inside the private root. |
| `audit-input --stage blind|compare --shard X --out-file F` | Exports the omission-audit input. |

## What `register_complete` means

The API boolean is true only when the validator finds no error, no pending cross-shard item and no skipped required check, and the register has been sealed — the state the sealed register reached on 2026-10-21. The boolean is mechanical: the validator checks structure, the wording accounting and the identifier ledger; it does not execute the financial rules the obligations describe and it does not evaluate any review. From checkpoint 38 on, the typed-link rule of item 8 is part of the mechanical predicate for the accepted bytes, claimed only after publication — the first seal's private-control mitigation stays the record for its exact historical packet and is not rewritten as if the old CLI enforced the new rule. Three different things must not be merged: this mechanical boolean, the program's own acceptance gates (every shard and the full register passed by the program's separate reviewer sessions with no open high or medium finding, the omission audit closed, every ambiguity carrying a decision owner), and the owner/fund content acceptance, which is still pending — as is full-system acceptance of GL-02 inside the product. A true `register_complete` means the mechanical predicate holds for the sealed register; with the recorded reviews the register is reported complete pending owner and fund review — never fund-approved — and no full live calculation run is claimed from rule inspections.

## For the owner and the fund

The review pack (`review_pack_sol_final_20261021/` in the canonical private root; the final pack is 11 files) has, per shard, a markdown file in sheet and row order and a workbook with the sheets Parents, Obligations, Ambiguities, CrossRow and Dispositions. Children labelled `analyst_inference` go to the fund first: they are what a competent analyst needs although the fund did not ask for it. Ambiguities carry a decision owner (36 logged records), and who resolves each is an owner/fund decision; their contents stay private. Sending anything to the fund is an owner action and is outside the coordinator's work.

## Known limits

- Uppercase words that look like a commercial coordinate in prose can be flagged by the reference rule; reword rather than cite.
- The typed-target gap MUT-APP01-001 is closed inside the validator from checkpoint 38 (item 8) for its accepted bytes, claimed only after publication; the first seal's private-control mitigation stays the record for its exact packet and is not rewritten as if the old CLI enforced the new rule.
- The register is a decomposition of what the checklist and the master plan require. Capability to answer each obligation is `unassessed` until GL-07 and GL-08 are read against it.
